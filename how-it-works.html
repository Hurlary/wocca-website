Home/How It Works

A three-stage path from claim to certificate.

Every water offset recognized by WOCCA passes through the same disciplined sequence, so a certificate always means the same thing.

THE METHODOLOGY

Measure, verify, certify — every time.

The sequence never changes. What varies is the project category, the evidence required, and the verifier assigned to review it.

01

Measure

The project developer selects the applicable WOCCA methodology, installs required metering, and documents baseline water use before the conservation activity begins.

02

Verify

An accredited, independent verifier reviews the data, inspects the site, and confirms the reported savings meet the methodology's requirements — including additionality.

03

Certify

WOCCA issues a serialized Water Offset Certificate for the verified volume, logs it in the public registry, and retires it once it's applied to a demand claim.

THE REGISTRY

One record, so nothing gets counted twice.

Every certificate carries a unique serial number tied to its project, methodology, and verification date. Once a certificate is applied to offset a specific demand, it's retired in the registry — permanently removing it from circulation.

The registry is publicly searchable, so regulators, buyers, and the public can confirm a certificate is valid before relying on it.

WHO'S INVOLVED

Four parties, one process.

Project developer

Designs and implements the conservation project and submits it for review.

Accredited verifier

Independently reviews project data and site evidence against the methodology.

Standards Committee

Maintains the methodologies and rules the whole process is measured against.

Public registry

Records every certificate issued and retired, open for anyone to check.

PROCESS FAQ

Before you get started.

How long does certification take?

Timelines depend on the project category and the monitoring period the methodology requires, since savings must be demonstrated, not just projected.

Who pays for verification?

Project developers engage and pay an accredited verifier directly; WOCCA does not perform verification itself, to keep the review independent.

Can a project lose its certification?

Certificates already issued for verified savings stand, but future certification for a project can be suspended if monitoring reveals it no longer meets the standard.
