OUR MISSION

Net-zero water, then water positive.

Water-scarce regions face a hard choice: slow growth, or risk overdrawing shared supply. WOCCA's mission is to remove that trade-off by giving every new source of water demand a credible path to being fully offset by verified conservation elsewhere in the same basin.

Net-zero water is the near-term target — new demand matched, gallon for gallon, by documented savings. Water positive is the destination: certified conservation that exceeds new demand, gradually easing pressure on the system as a whole.

OUR STANDARDS

Methodologies for how water gets saved.

Each project category has its own approved methodology defining how savings are calculated, what evidence is required, and how long the outcome must be sustained to qualify.

Irrigation efficiency

Converting flood or spray irrigation to drip systems, precision scheduling, and soil-moisture monitoring.

Leak detection & repair

Distribution-system audits and infrastructure repair that reduce non-revenue water loss.

Greywater & recycling

On-site treatment and reuse systems that displace potable water in non-drinking applications.

Stormwater capture

Catchment and storage projects that recharge aquifers or offset municipal supply during dry periods.

STANDARDS FAQ

Common questions about offset integrity.

What does "additionality" mean?

A project is additional if the water savings would not have happened without WOCCA certification as an incentive — conservation already required by law or existing contracts does not qualify.

How is double-counting prevented?

Every certificate is issued once, recorded in the public registry, and retired at the point of use, so the same unit of saved water can never back two separate claims.

What happens if a project underperforms?

Certificates are only issued for savings that have already been verified. Ongoing projects are subject to periodic re-verification before further certificates are released.

Do standards ever change?

Yes. Methodologies are revisited on a set cycle and updated through an open comment process as monitoring technology and hydrological science improve.
